the problem

A huge community, and very few tools built for them.

Pakistan is home to an estimated 10 million Deaf and hard-of-hearing people, yet Pakistan Sign Language interpreters, accessible learning tools, and everyday communication aids remain scarce. That gap shows up early: fewer than 5% of deaf children in Pakistan attend school, largely because of communication barriers, not ability.

under the hood

The model, the pipeline, the numbers.

SignRead is trained on three public Kaggle datasets covering ASL letters and digits, using transfer learning on top of an ImageNet-pretrained backbone, then compressed and converted to run entirely client-side.

Model architecture

EfficientNet-B0 (pretrained on ImageNet) with a custom classification head, two linear layers with dropout, fine-tuned in PyTorch on 224×224 hand images.

Training data

ASL Alphabet, Sign Language MNIST, and American Sign Language 0–9 + A–Z, combined and split into train/validation/test sets, ~36 balanced classes.

Deployment

Exported to ONNX and served with ONNX Runtime Web (WebAssembly): the entire model runs inside your browser tab, with zero backend server.

Tooling

Python, PyTorch, torchvision, scikit-learn for evaluation, and a hand-built camera interface in vanilla HTML/JS for the live demo.

Being honest about the numbers: digits lag behind letters mainly because our digit training data came from a single, smaller dataset, while letters drew on two much larger ones. Several ASL digits also share hand shapes with letters (like 1 and D, or 2 and V), which the model still confuses at times. Expanding digit training data is one of our next concrete steps.
where we're headed

From letters, to words, to real conversation.

Recognizing individual signs is the foundation. Here's the path we're building toward next, in order.

01

Word & sentence recognition

Move beyond single letters and digits to recognize full signed words and phrases as continuous motion, not just static hand shapes.

02

Sentence construction

Automatically arrange recognized signs into grammatically correct sentences, rather than a raw string of words.

03

Text or speech output (your choice)

Let the user choose how they want the translated sentence delivered: as readable text, or spoken aloud through text-to-speech.

04

Two-way communication

Go in reverse too: convert typed or spoken language back into sign animations, so a conversation can flow in both directions.

05

A dedicated mobile app

Bring SignRead out of the browser and into a proper iOS and Android app, built for everyday, on-the-go use.

06

Multiple sign languages

Extend support beyond ASL to other sign languages, including Pakistan Sign Language, so more communities can benefit.
